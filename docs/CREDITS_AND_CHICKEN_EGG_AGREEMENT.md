## 2. Purpose: the Chicken-or-Egg principle

Creative and technical work often develops reciprocally. A concept may guide an implementation; a prototype may expose a missing concept; tests and research may change both. The project will not force a false claim that one always came first.

For each material feature, distinguish:

- **Concept record:** dated notes, sketches, drafts, prompts, research questions, diagrams, or other design evidence.
- **Implementation record:** source files, commits, prototypes, tests, model configurations, builds, and reproducible outputs.
- **Feedback relationship:** whether implementation realizes, refines, challenges, constrains, or inspires the concept.
- **Uncertainty:** missing records, conflicting chronology, incomplete archives, or disputed interpretation.

Where the record establishes priority, state it with the supporting evidence. Where the work is iterative or chronology is not established, describe reciprocal development and do not invent a first cause.

## 3. Forensic preservation and attribution

1. Preserve original artifacts and meaningful version history. Work from copies when analyzing evidence.
2. Record repository, path, commit SHA, immutable model or dataset revision, retrieval time, and SHA-256 digest where available.
3. Keep conceptual contributions separate from coding, testing, research, documentation, review, and maintenance.
4. Credit actual contributions only where supported by source records or a contributor's confirmed statement.
5. A hash proves byte identity, not authorship or creation date. A commit records repository history, not necessarily legal authorship. A passing test proves only the behavior tested.
6. Label evidence **Verified**, **Corroborated**, **User-reported**, **Inferred**, **Proposed**, **Disputed**, or **Unknown**.
7. Do not backdate, fabricate, silently overwrite, or selectively omit material evidence.
8. Correct errors transparently and retain enough history to understand the correction.
9. Do not infer consent, signature, employment, family relationship, ownership share, or endorsement from a credit entry.
10. If evidence is incomplete, state the limitation rather than converting uncertainty into a definitive claim.

## 5. Dispute and correction process

When a material attribution or chronology dispute arises:

1. State the exact disputed claim without embellishment.
2. Identify the primary evidence and its limitations.
3. Invite relevant contributors to provide dated source material or a correction.
4. Preserve conflicting accounts without silently choosing one.
5. Publish a correction or neutral status when the evidence supports it.
6. Escalate ownership or legal disputes to qualified counsel rather than treating this working agreement as a legal determination.
